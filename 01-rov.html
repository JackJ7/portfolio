ROV — CRUSH DEPTH

Underwater remotely operated vehicle — MATE ROV World Championship

REV A
2026-08-27
INDEX 01 ROV 02 PERRY

1OVERVIEW

Crush Depth's ROV is an underwater remotely operated vehicle built for the MATE ROV competition, where it performs simulated environmental research and industrial inspection tasks. The team — roughly 47 members of Clovis Community College's Engineering Renaissance Club — competed at the MATE ROV World Championship in 2025 and 2026.

2MECHANICAL DESIGN

The vehicle's structure is custom, printed FDM on Bambu Lab machines and modeled in SolidWorks: an outer chassis, an internal housing that carries the electronics stack, and a TPU foam ballast printed to shape. A custom manipulator, designed against the competition's mission tasks, is the vehicle's working end.

Waterproofing is handled as a vehicle-level strategy rather than a per-part fix — every penetration, housing, and connector has to hold at mission depth for the vehicle to score at all.

3ELECTRONICS & POWER

A Raspberry Pi 5 is the vehicle computer, driving thrusters through ESCs via a PCA9685 PWM driver, with an MPU6050 IMU for attitude sensing and camera systems for the pilot. Power distribution runs through multiple buck converters stepping the tether supply down to the rails the electronics need.

4CONTROL SOFTWARE

The control architecture (C++) is designed around pilot ergonomics: the layout of controls and behaviors is chosen for ease of piloting under mission time pressure, rather than for a clean mapping to actuators. Mission scores depend on what the pilot can actually execute.

5ROLE & ATTRIBUTION

The author is Co-CEO / CTO of Crush Depth, co-managing the team with Luke Salazar, and owns the electrical and software domains — promoted from Float Team Technical Lead. Operations at this scale are their own engineering problem: Gantt scheduling, BOM management, and a leadership structure deliberately modeled on a company per MATE guidelines.

At the 2026 World Championship the author won the Engineering Presentation MVP award.

6KNOWN LIMITATIONS

  • The vehicle's total power budget was never formally characterized; converter sizing was validated by operation, not analysis.
  • Manipulator payload capacity is unquantified.
  • The hardware is currently not accessible to the author; figures on this sheet are limited to what was documented at build time.

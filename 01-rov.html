Crush Depth ROV: Mechanical, Electrical, and Control Design of a Competition Remotely Operated Vehicle

MATE ROV World Championship, 2025 and 2026
J. O. JacksonCrush Depth, Engineering Renaissance Club, Clovis Community College

Abstract. Crush Depth's ROV is an underwater remotely operated vehicle built for the MATE ROV competition, where it performs simulated environmental-research and industrial-inspection tasks. The structure is custom, modeled in SolidWorks and FDM-printed: an outer chassis, an internal housing for the electronics stack, a TPU foam ballast printed to shape, and a manipulator designed against the mission tasks. A Raspberry Pi 5 drives the thrusters through ESCs and a PCA9685 PWM driver, with an MPU6050 IMU for attitude and cameras for the pilot; power is stepped down from the tether through multiple buck converters. The C++ control architecture is laid out for pilot ergonomics under mission time pressure. The team competed at the World Championship in 2025 and 2026; the author received the Engineering Presentation MVP award in 2026. Unquantified aspects of the design are stated in Section 6.

Keywords: remotely operated vehicle, FDM chassis, waterproofing, manipulator, Raspberry Pi 5, PCA9685, pilot ergonomics

1Introduction

The MATE ROV competition scores a vehicle on mission tasks performed underwater by a pilot at the surface. The team, roughly 47 members of Clovis Community College's Engineering Renaissance Club, competed at the MATE ROV World Championship in 2025 and 2026. This memorandum records the vehicle's mechanical structure, its electrical and power system, and the control architecture, and states what was and was not characterized.

2Mechanical Design

The vehicle's structure is custom, modeled in SolidWorks and printed FDM on Bambu Lab machines: an outer chassis, an internal housing that carries the electronics stack, and a TPU foam ballast printed to shape. A custom manipulator, designed against the competition's mission tasks, is the vehicle's working end.

Waterproofing is handled as a vehicle-level strategy rather than a per-part fix. Every penetration, housing, and connector has to hold at mission depth for the vehicle to score at all.

3Electronics and Power

A Raspberry Pi 5 is the vehicle computer. It drives the thrusters through ESCs via a PCA9685 PWM driver, reads an MPU6050 IMU for attitude sensing, and carries the camera systems for the pilot. Power distribution runs through multiple buck converters stepping the tether supply down to the rails the electronics need.

4Control Software

The control architecture, written in C++, is designed around pilot ergonomics: the layout of controls and behaviors is chosen for ease of piloting under mission time pressure, rather than for a clean mapping to actuators. Mission scores depend on what the pilot can actually execute.

5Team and Role

The author is Co-CEO / CTO of Crush Depth, co-managing the team with Luke Salazar, and owns the electrical and software domains, promoted from Float Team Technical Lead. Operations at this scale are their own engineering problem: Gantt scheduling, BOM management, and a leadership structure deliberately modeled on a company per MATE guidelines.

At the 2026 World Championship the author won the Engineering Presentation MVP award.

6Known Limitations

  • The vehicle's total power budget was never formally characterized; converter sizing was validated by operation, not analysis.
  • Manipulator payload capacity is unquantified.
  • The hardware is currently not accessible to the author; figures in this memorandum are limited to what was documented at build time.

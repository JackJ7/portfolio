PERRY

Pressure Equipped Robot for Research and Yield — vertical profiling float

REV A
2026-08-27
INDEX 01 ROV 02 PERRY

1OVERVIEW

PERRY is a vertical profiling float built for the MATE ROV international competition, modeled on the oceanographic float craft used for ocean-health and weather monitoring. On receiving an activation signal over LoRa, it executes three vertical profiles to a predetermined depth, logging temperature, pressure, and depth throughout, then transmits the full dataset to the base station on resurfacing.

The craft completed its mission profile in open water: it profiled to 4 m, resurfaced, and delivered its logged data over the radio link.

2PRESSURE SEALING

The hull is a sealed pressure vessel and the sealing strategy is the core mechanical problem of the craft. Static o-ring seals close the hull; seal design covered o-ring groove sizing, seal-stack layout, and selection of a waterproof grease for assembly.

The hull is FDM-printed in ABS, which leaves a layered, porous surface unsuited to sealing. Printed sealing surfaces were treated by acetone vapor smoothing, using the solvent pass as a conformal surface treatment to close layer lines before seal assembly.

3BUOYANCY ENGINE

Depth is driven by a buoyancy engine rather than thrusters: a NEMA17 stepper motor, running through a TMC2209 driver under UART control, changes the craft's displacement to move it between the surface and the target depth. Sizing the engine against the craft's buoyancy budget — the physics side of the design — sat with the author, alongside the electronics and firmware.

4ELECTRONICS & FIRMWARE

An ESP32 runs the mission logic: arming over LoRa, profile sequencing, sensor logging, and data return. Depth and pressure come from a Blue Robotics Bar02 pressure sensor on the I²C bus. The stepper driver is commanded over UART. All electronics were assembled and soldered on protoboard.

5ROLE & ATTRIBUTION

Team project (Crush Depth float team). The author owned the programming, the electronics, and the physics and buoyancy side of the craft, and previously served as Float Team Technical Lead — taking the subteam through LoRa communications, ESP32 development, stepper motors and drivers, and board assembly.

6KNOWN LIMITATIONS

  • Seal integrity is verified to 4 m only. Maximum safe operating depth has not been characterized.
  • Effective LoRa range was not measured; the link is verified only at mission distances.
  • Craft mass, displacement, and sensor sample rate were not formally recorded.
  • Single Bar02 sensor with no redundancy; a sensor fault ends the mission.

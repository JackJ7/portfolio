PERRY: Design and Open-Water Verification of a LoRa-Activated Vertical Profiling Float

Pressure Equipped Robot for Research and Yield
J. O. JacksonCrush Depth float team, MATE ROV international competition

Abstract. PERRY is a vertical profiling float modeled on the oceanographic floats used for ocean-health and weather monitoring. On an activation signal received over LoRa, the craft executes three vertical profiles to a predetermined depth while logging temperature, pressure, and depth, then transmits the dataset to a base station on resurfacing. The hull is an FDM-printed ABS pressure vessel closed by static o-ring seals; printed sealing surfaces were acetone-vapor smoothed to close layer lines. Depth is driven by a stepper-actuated buoyancy engine rather than thrusters. In open water the craft profiled to 4 m, resurfaced, and returned its logged data over the radio link. Limits of the verification are stated in Section 6.

Keywords: profiling float, o-ring seal design, buoyancy engine, FDM ABS, ESP32, LoRa

1Introduction

Profiling floats measure the water column by cycling between the surface and a target depth under their own buoyancy control. PERRY reproduces that mission at competition scale. The design problem divides into three parts: a hull that seals reliably under pressure, a buoyancy engine that moves the craft vertically without thrusters, and a radio-linked controller that sequences the mission and returns the data.

2Pressure Sealing

The hull is a sealed pressure vessel and the sealing strategy is the core mechanical problem of the craft. Static o-ring seals close the hull. The seal design covered o-ring groove sizing, seal-stack layout, and selection of a waterproof grease for assembly.

The hull is FDM-printed in ABS on a Bambu Lab printer, which leaves a layered, porous surface unsuited to sealing. Printed sealing surfaces were treated by acetone vapor smoothing, using the solvent pass as a conformal surface treatment to close layer lines before seal assembly.

3Buoyancy Engine

Depth is driven by a buoyancy engine: a NEMA17 stepper motor, running through a TMC2209 driver under UART control, changes the craft's displacement to move it between the surface and the target depth. Sizing the engine against the craft's buoyancy budget sat with the author, alongside the electronics and firmware.

4Electronics and Firmware

An ESP32 runs the mission logic: arming over LoRa, profile sequencing, sensor logging, and data return. Depth and pressure are read from a Blue Robotics Bar02 pressure sensor on the I²C bus. The stepper driver is commanded over UART. All electronics were assembled and soldered on protoboard.

5Open-Water Verification

The craft completed its mission profile in open water: it profiled to 4 m, resurfaced, and delivered its logged data over the radio link. The depth-versus-time record for the mission is to be plotted from the exported Bar02 log rather than drawn by hand.

6Known Limitations

  • Seal integrity is verified to 4 m only. Maximum safe operating depth has not been characterized.
  • Effective LoRa range was not measured; the link is verified only at mission distances.
  • Craft mass, displacement, and sensor sample rate were not formally recorded.
  • Single Bar02 sensor with no redundancy; a sensor fault ends the mission.
